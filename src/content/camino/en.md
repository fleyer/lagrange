The Camino de Santiago (the Way of St. James) refers to the network of routes leading to the cathedral of Santiago de Compostela in Galicia, Spain. Éauze lies on one of the most walked of these routes, the Via Podiensis, which crosses the Gers department from east to west on its way toward the Pyrenees.

<h2 id="voie">The Via Podiensis</h2>

The Via Podiensis begins in Le Puy-en-Velay, in the Haute-Loire, and runs roughly 740 km to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port at the foot of the Pyrenees, where it joins the Spanish Camino Francés. It is marked with the GR65 waymark — red and white stripes painted on posts, walls and trees — alongside the yellow scallop shell on blue background used across all Camino de Santiago routes. Through Gascony, the path crosses small market towns, Armagnac vineyards and medieval bastide towns before reaching the Pyrenean foothills.

<h2 id="etapes">Éauze on the route</h2>

Éauze sits between two classic stages of the Via Podiensis. Coming from the east, pilgrims usually reach Éauze from Montréal-du-Gers (about 17 km), after passing through Condom the day or two before. Heading on toward the Pyrenees, the next stage leads to Nogaro (about 20 km), before Aire-sur-l'Adour. Some walkers cover Condom–Éauze in a single long day (roughly 35 km) — an option best left to experienced pilgrims, and worth double-checking against a current topo-guide before setting out, as distances can vary slightly depending on the exact variant followed.

<h2 id="pratique">Practical information</h2>

Every pilgrim carries a créanciale — the pilgrim's passport, stamped at each stage (tourist offices, gîtes, churches) to record the journey and required to receive the Compostela certificate on arrival in Santiago. The Gers stages are best walked between March and October, avoiding the peak summer heat. Several local operators offer stage-to-stage luggage transfer for pilgrims who want to walk lighter.

<h2 id="sejour">Resting at La Grange</h2>

In Éauze, the gîte La Grange de Marie-France welcomes walkers for a restful night before setting off again: quiet rooms, shared meals, and a welcome attentive to pilgrims' needs.
