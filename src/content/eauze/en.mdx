Éauze is a small Gascon town in the Gers, built on a ridge above the Auzoue valley. A stage on the Via Podiensis — the Camino de Santiago route starting in Le Puy-en-Velay — it welcomes pilgrims from across Europe every year, and long before them, it was one of the great cities of Roman Gaul.

<h2 id="histoire">Elusa, a Gallo-Roman city</h2>

Under its ancient name, Elusa, the town was the capital of the Elusates, an Aquitanian people, before becoming the capital of the Roman province of Novempopulana — a territory roughly covering today's Gascony. The city prospered for centuries, with public monuments whose traces still turn up in archaeological digs in the town center.

<SideBySide src={treasureImage} alt="Coin hoard from Elusa">
On 18 October 1985, a construction site uncovered one of the largest Roman coin hoards ever found in France: nearly 28,000 silver and bronze coins, along with some fifty pieces of jewelry, buried after 261 AD during the Crisis of the Third Century. The hoard is now displayed at the Musée archéologique du Trésor in the heart of Éauze, alongside other remains of the Elusa site, such as the Villa de Séviac and its mosaics.
</SideBySide>

Éauze also calls itself the capital of Bas-Armagnac, one of the three production zones for Armagnac brandy, France's oldest eau-de-vie. The surrounding hills are covered in vineyards, and several family-run estates still offer cellar visits and tastings — a welcome detour for walkers looking to extend their day with a taste of the local terroir.

<h2 id="commodites">What pilgrims will find</h2>

For anyone arriving on foot after a long day's walk, Éauze has the essentials: bakeries, a supermarket, a pharmacy, several restaurants and bars, a cash machine, and a bus stop for regional connections. The central square and its covered market hall, lined with half-timbered houses, make a good place to rest before continuing on toward Nogaro or back toward Montréal-du-Gers and Condom.

To cool off in summer, the municipal swimming pool is open from June to September. And every Thursday morning, the weekly market fills the town centre with local produce stalls.

<h2 id="hebergement">Staying in Éauze</h2>

A hundred meters from the town center, the gîte La Grange de Marie-France welcomes pilgrims for a restful night: quiet rooms, shared meals, and a warm welcome designed for those walking to Santiago.
